Thoughts arrive on their own. You don't get to choose which boats come in, only whether you climb aboard.

The idea

Mind lake turns a classic observing-the-mind practice into a picture anyone can hold. You sit on the shore. Each thought is a boat with a colored sail. Some are light and bright, some are heavy. All of them sail on if you let them.

Know your boats

Thirteen kinds of boats carry more than a hundred everyday thoughts about friends, family, school, and ourselves, just like a real busy mind. Naming the color of a thought creates a little space between you and it.

Light boats

Notice them, enjoy them, and let them sail on too.

Drifting boats

Silly, busy, and dreamy thoughts. No need to do anything at all.

Heavy boats

Don't climb aboard. Breathe slowly and watch them leave.

How a sit works

Ninety seconds, four simple moves. There's no way to lose, only ways to come back to the shore.

  1. Sit

    You are the child on the sand. The boats are not you. They simply pass through your view.

  2. Notice

    Tap a light or drifting boat to notice it. Your calm grows a little, and the boat keeps sailing.

  3. Breathe

    When a heavy boat drifts toward the shore, hold the breath button until the ring fills.

  4. Let go

    Release to breathe out. A soft breeze carries heavy boats back to sea and out of sight.

The 90-second sit

A short guided game. Heavy boats drift toward the shore, and a slow breath sends them back out. The sky brightens as your calm grows.

Best for a reset during the day, before school, or after a hard moment.

Just watch

No timer and no score. Each boat shows the thought it carries, and you simply watch it come and go across the water.

Best for bedtime, a quiet minute together, or longer meditation.

Talk about it together

After a sit, a few gentle questions help children put feelings into words.

  • What color boat is on your lake right now?Naming the boat gives a little space from it.
  • Did a heavy boat try to dock today?Ask without fixing. Just listen.
  • What helped it sail away?A breath, a hug, a walk, a glass of water.
  • Which light boat would you like to see more of?Kindness and friendship can be invited.

90s

Why ninety seconds

A feeling often rises and falls like a wave. If we stop feeding it with more thoughts, its physical rush can pass in about a minute and a half. The idea was popularized by neuroanatomist Jill Bolte Taylor, and Mind lake turns it into a picture: the boat crosses, and the water goes still again.
